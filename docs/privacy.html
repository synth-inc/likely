Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 12, 2026

Likely is built so that very little leaves your Mac. The app doesn’t run an AI of its own and never uploads your files to us. The app does send us usage analytics, which you can turn off, but never your searches, file names or files. Our license server keeps records and analytics about the requests it receives, Stripe sends us details of purchases, and our website uses advertising cookies to measure our ads. This policy explains what leaves your Mac, where it goes, what we keep, and how to delete it.

1. Who we are

Likely is a macOS menu bar app made by Synthetic Exploration, Inc., a Delaware corporation (“Synth”, “we”, “us”). This policy covers the Likely app, its license and update services, and this website (synth-inc.github.io/likely). Our Terms of Service cover your use of the paid app and services.

Likely’s source code is public under the GPL-3.0 license at github.com/synth-inc/likely, so you can check what the app does against the code.

2. What we collect, and what we don’t

We (Synth) receive only what we need to sell and license the app and to keep our server running, and what you choose to send us:

  • An anonymous account and install ID. When you continue past Likely’s first setup screen, before you buy anything, Likely creates an anonymous account on our license server. The account is identified by a random ID created when you install Likely (the install ID), which isn’t derived from your hardware, name or files. The app sends that ID and the app’s identifier (“likely”) when it signs in, and checks your license at launch and about every 12 hours. Builds you make yourself with the free option don’t contact our server.
  • Purchase and license records. There’s no sign-up or sign-in. If you buy a license, our server links the purchase to your account and keeps the plan, whether the license is active, when a subscription renews or ends, and Stripe’s reference numbers for the purchase (customer, checkout, payment and subscription IDs). Your account record itself doesn’t include your email address, but the Stripe records below do.
  • Purchase details from Stripe. When you buy, renew, cancel or get a refund, Stripe sends our server a notification, and we store each one exactly as Stripe sends it. Depending on the event, that includes the email address, name and phone number (if collected) you enter at checkout, your billing address, and payment details such as your card’s brand, last 4 digits, expiry date, issuing country and a card fingerprint, plus Stripe’s fraud checks on the payment. It never includes your full card number or security code. Copies of each notification are also kept in our server’s job queue (Redis Cloud) and, if processing one fails, written in full to our server logs. We can also see these details and your purchase history in our Stripe account, for example to answer a support or refund request.
  • Network information. Like any web server, ours (and GitHub, which hosts downloads and this site) sees your IP address when the app or your browser connects.
  • Server analytics. Our license server sends a record of every request it handles, including license checks and update checks, to PostHog, our analytics provider. Each record includes the address requested, the response status and how long it took, your account number on our server (when the app is signed in), your IP address, and the user agent (the app’s name and version and, for license checks, version numbers of macOS components). Errors on our server are recorded the same way. When the app signs in, PostHog is also told your account number, so the records can be grouped by account. We use this to keep the server reliable, find problems, and understand how the service is used.
  • App usage analytics. Unless you turn off “Share usage analytics” in Settings, the app sends PostHog records of how it’s used, linked to your install ID. These include things like your app and macOS versions, setup, how searches perform (the agent and model, timing, results and which tool found them), tools’ names, IDs and commands and whether they help, settings, test results, and errors (including the app a problem happened in). They never include what you type in searches, window titles, file names, paths or file contents, or tools’ instructions. We use them to fix problems and improve Likely, including its default tools.
  • Website visits and ad measurement. Our website uses advertising pixels from partners such as Reddit to measure which ads bring people to Likely, for example whether a visit from an ad led to a download. See Our website and advertising.
  • What you send us. If you email us or file a GitHub issue, we get what you send, which may include a log you attach. A detailed log (“Log details for troubleshooting”) can contain your searches, window titles and file paths. We use it only to answer you and fix the problem. GitHub issues are public, so please don’t attach a log with anything sensitive there. If you need to share one, email it to us instead. We keep support emails and attachments as long as we need them to help you, and delete them when you ask.

We don’t collect:

  • your files, file names, paths or folder contents, or window titles;
  • your searches or search history, unless you send them to us (see “What you send us” above);
  • what your AI provider sends back;
  • your full card number or security code, which go to Stripe and never reach us.

We don’t sell personal information for money, and we don’t use it to train AI models. Our website does share browsing activity with advertising partners, which US state privacy laws may treat as “sharing” for cross-context behavioral advertising; you can opt out (see Our website and advertising). The app itself shares nothing with advertising partners.

4. AI providers

Likely doesn’t run an AI. When you type a search, Likely starts the coding agent you chose in Settings (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, OpenCode or Hermes) on your Mac. The agent runs under your own account with that provider (or, for OpenCode’s free models, under OpenCode’s terms with no account) and searches with Spotlight and read-only tools, plus any tools you add. Likely then copies the files it suggests into your Likely folder.

What the agent sends to its provider is governed by your agreement with that provider and its privacy terms, not by this policy. Your provider isn’t our processor: we don’t receive your searches or the provider’s answers, and you and your provider decide how they’re handled. Likely reads only the file suggestions the agent prints, on your Mac.

Your provider may train on what you send. Depending on the provider and your plan, consumer AI plans and free models may use your searches and file contents to train or improve their models. Check your provider’s privacy settings and terms; many let you turn this off in your account settings.

OpenCode’s free model. OpenCode’s default free model needs no account. Searches you make with it are handled under OpenCode’s terms and privacy policy, and free models are among those that may use what you send to improve them.

Likely uses its own agent settings. Likely starts your agent with its own settings, so options in your agent’s settings files, such as telemetry or data-sharing switches, may not apply to Likely’s searches. Settings in your provider account do apply. Gemini CLI runs through Vertex AI, under your Google Cloud project’s terms.

You control what goes to the provider: your name has a switch in Settings, the agent only searches when you type, and you choose which tools to add and what your prompt says.

5. Payment

Purchases go through Stripe Checkout, and subscriptions are managed in Stripe’s billing portal. Stripe processes your payment under its own privacy policy. Your full card number and security code go to Stripe and never reach us. Stripe tells our server when a purchase, renewal, cancellation or refund happens, so we can turn your license on or off. We store each of those notifications exactly as Stripe sends it (what they contain is listed in section 2), and copies pass through our job queue (Redis Cloud) and, when processing fails, our server logs. We use them to run your license, handle refunds and support, and keep accounting records. We can also see your email and purchase history in our Stripe account.

6. Our service providers

These companies process personal information for us, for these purposes only:

  • Heroku (Salesforce) hosts our license server, its database and its logs.
  • Stripe processes payments and manages subscriptions. Stripe is also an independent controller for some of its own processing, such as fraud prevention, under its privacy policy.
  • PostHog receives our server’s analytics (a record of each request) and the app’s usage analytics, both described in section 2.
  • Redis Cloud (Redis Ltd., through Heroku) runs our server’s job queue, which holds each notification Stripe sends us, in full, while our server processes it.
  • GitHub hosts Likely’s downloads, its source code and this website, and sees your IP address when you visit or download.

Your AI provider isn’t on this list because it doesn’t work for us: Likely sends your searches to it from your Mac, under your own agreement with it.

7. Updates

Paid builds of Likely check for updates with Sparkle. The update feed is on our server and the download comes from GitHub releases, so both see your IP address and app version, and requests to the feed are recorded in our analytics. Sparkle’s optional system-profile reporting is off, so no other information about your Mac is sent with the check. Sparkle asks before installing an update unless you choose to install updates automatically, and you can turn off automatic checks in Settings. Builds you make yourself from source, free or dev, don’t check for updates.

8. Our website and advertising

GitHub hosts this website and sees your IP address when you visit. To learn which of our ads work, the website also uses advertising pixels from partners such as Reddit. A pixel is a small script that tells the partner you visited a page or clicked Download. It sets cookies and sends the partner your IP address, browser and device details, the page and what you clicked, and the partner’s own cookie IDs, which it may link to your account with it. Each partner handles this under its own privacy policy (for Reddit, see Reddit’s Privacy Policy), and may use it to show you ads elsewhere.

Opting out. If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, or you turn them off below, we don’t load advertising pixels, and we treat that as a request to opt out of sale and sharing. Where the law requires your consent before these cookies are set, we ask for it first, and you can withdraw it below at any time. You can also block them in your browser or with a content blocker, or change ad settings in your account with the partner.

The Likely app contains no advertising pixels and sends nothing to advertising partners.

9. Data on your Mac, and how to delete it

Likely keeps its working data on your Mac, where we can’t see it:

  • ~/Likely (or the folder you chose): copies of suggested files. Copies are cloned locally and cleared shortly after the picker closes; your originals are never changed.
  • ~/Library/Application Support/Likely: search history (History, on by default: the last 500 searches with the app, window title, what you typed, suggestions and what you picked; encrypted in our releases; turn it off or clear it in Settings → History), your tools (tools.json), eval cases and results (evals), and your agent’s working folder (run).
  • ~/Library/Logs/Likely: Likely’s log, which may mention past searches.
  • Likely’s preferences (inc.synth.likely), which hold your settings, the install ID and your cached license status, and a Keychain item (inc.synth.likely.server) holding an access token for the license server.

To delete search history, use Settings → History. To delete all of Likely’s data on your Mac, quit Likely and delete ~/Library/Application Support/Likely, ~/Library/Application Support/Likely.noindex, ~/Library/Logs/Likely and ~/Library/Logs/Likely.noindex (the folders above are links to the .noindex ones), then run defaults delete inc.synth.likely in Terminal and remove the Keychain item in Keychain Access. Likely also keeps an encryption key for your search history in your Mac’s data-protection keychain, which Keychain Access doesn’t show; without the data, it protects nothing. Deleting Likely’s preferences also removes your license activation from this Mac; email us to restore it. Earlier versions of Likely could save screenshots; Likely deletes any they left when it starts. Your AI provider’s CLI may keep its own session logs; see its documentation.

10. Retention

We keep each kind of record only as long as we need it:

  • Account and license records (install ID, plan, license status and Stripe’s purchase IDs): for buyers, while your license is active and then for 7 years for tax, accounting and disputes. If you never buy, your anonymous account is deleted 90 days after the app last contacted our server.
  • Stripe notifications we store: 7 years, for tax and accounting. Stripe keeps its own payment records under its own policy.
  • Stripe notifications in our job queue (Redis Cloud): kept indefinitely.
  • Analytics records in PostHog: kept indefinitely.
  • Website pixel data: kept by each advertising partner under its own policy.
  • Server logs (which include IP addresses, and the full Stripe notification when processing one fails): kept indefinitely.

When you ask us to delete your information, we complete the request within 30 days. The only exception is purchase records we must keep by law, such as tax records, which we then keep only for that purpose and only for as long as the law requires.

11. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to know what personal information we hold about you and to access, correct, delete or export it, or to object to or limit how we use it. To make a request, including asking us to delete your account, license and purchase records, email contact@cloudless.so from the address you used at checkout, or include your receipt number. We use these to find your records and confirm the request is yours. If you never bought a license, your anonymous account holds only a random install ID, and our server records and analytics hold IP addresses linked to it. We delete them on the schedule in Retention. If you want them deleted sooner, or want a copy, email us, and we’ll tell you how to find your install ID so we can find your records.

We’ll respond within 30 days. If we decline any part of a request, we’ll tell you why; you can appeal by replying “appeal”, and we’ll review the decision and answer within 30 days. If you’re still unhappy, you can complain to your state attorney general or your local data protection authority.

You can ask someone else (an authorized agent) to make a request for you. We may ask for proof that they’re allowed to, and ask you to confirm the request. We won’t treat you differently, for example by charging you more or giving you a lesser service, because you used any of these rights.

Opting out of sharing, and Global Privacy Control. To opt out of the sharing described in Our website and advertising, use “Turn off advertising cookies” in section 8, or turn on Global Privacy Control in your browser; either way we don’t load advertising pixels. There’s no single standard for Do Not Track signals, so we don’t respond to them; we do honor Global Privacy Control.

Data on your Mac is already under your control; you can delete it at any time as described above.

14. Security and breaches

The app talks to our server only over HTTPS. If a breach affects your personal information, we’ll tell you without undue delay, by email if we have your address and on this website, and we’ll notify the authorities as the law requires.

15. If Likely changes hands

If Synth or Likely is sold, merged or otherwise transferred, the new owner must honor this policy for information collected under it. We’ll tell you before your information is transferred, and you can ask us to delete it first.

Likely’s license server is shared with another Synth product, Onit. Likely’s records are used only for Likely, not for our other products.

16. Children

Likely isn’t directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect personal information from them. If you’re 13 to 17, or otherwise under the age of majority where you live, you need a parent or guardian to agree to our Terms for you and to make any purchase. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, contact us and we’ll delete it.

17. Changes

If we change this policy, we’ll post the new version here and update the effective date. If a change is material, for example if Likely starts sending a new kind of information off your Mac, we’ll tell you in the app, and by email if we have your address, at least 30 days before it takes effect. We won’t use information we already hold in a materially new way unless you first opt in.
